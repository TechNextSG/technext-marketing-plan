Email marketing, Singapore only.

Everything needed to send is already paid for and working. What is missing is a Singapore list, a reason to reply, and a rule about volume. This page is those three things.

The whole plan in one sentence. Send small, researched, personal emails to Singapore SMEs about their IRAS InvoiceNow deadline — capped at 11 a day so the Spam Control Act's <ADV> rule never applies — and build an opted-in list alongside it for everything else.

Domain authenticated 20,000 sends/month paid for 3 templates built Cold cap 11/day Singapore leads 0 queued

1 The plan

Four decisions. Everything else on this page follows from them.

1

Send from technextasia.com

It has SPF, both Brevo DKIM keys and a p=quarantine DMARC policy, and a real delivered message was checked passing all three. The look-alike technext.asia is the website — it has no SPF, so nothing marketing-related sends from it.

2

Lead with InvoiceNow, not with TechNext

IRAS has set dated, non-optional e-invoicing deadlines for GST-registered businesses. Almost no SME knows their own date. That is a better opening line than anything we could write about ourselves.

3

Two lanes that never mix

Cold stays small, plain and personal, under the legal bulk threshold. Volume only ever goes to people who opted in. The 20,000/month capacity belongs entirely to lane 2.

4

Build the Singapore list first

There are ~3,900 scraped Singapore rows sitting in Brevo in no list, and they are not opted in. The first deliverable is 120 researched rows with a named contact and a checkable trigger — not a bigger scrape.

The old blocker is gone. Every earlier TechNext document lists "no SPF" as the reason email never launched. technextasia.com now publishes v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com include:spf.brevo.com ~all, both brevo1/brevo2._domainkey CNAMEs and Google DKIM. There is no infrastructure reason left to wait.

3 The reason to reply

Singapore is phasing in mandatory transmission of invoice data to IRAS over the InvoiceNow (Peppol) network. Each business has a different start date. That gap is the whole cold-email premise.

⚠ Two things the copy must never do.
  • Never state a specific date for a specific company. Bands depend on total annual supplies, and IRAS said it would notify existing registrants individually. Write "your date depends on your turnover band, and we confirm it in scoping."
  • Never imply grant funding. Whether TechNext is a pre-approved PSG or EDG vendor is unverified. Until that is confirmed in writing, say "we can point you to the current schemes" and nothing more.
Schedule as read from IRAS guidance on 31 July 2026 — re-read it before each campaign, it is the one thing here most likely to move.

4 The Singapore rules

Two different statutes, two different tests. Getting this right is what lets us email cold at all. Operating rules, not legal advice.

PDPA Business contact information is exempt

A person's name, title, business phone, business address and business email — where not given solely for personal purposes — sit outside the consent requirement. The Do Not Call Registry covers phone, SMS and fax, not email.

OK Emailing ops@company.com.sg about their business, no prior consent needed.
CARE Personal-looking addresses on free mail domains — treat as personal data and leave them out.

Spam Control Act Unsolicited and in bulk triggers hard duties

Once a commercial message is both unsolicited and sent in bulk, the subject line must begin with <ADV> followed by a space, plus an English unsubscribe statement, a working reply address, truthful headers, a postal address, an opt-out live for at least 30 days, and removal within 10 business days.

"In bulk" has numbers: more than 100 messages in 24 hours, 1,000 in 30 days, or 10,000 in a year on the same or similar subject matter.

The arithmetic that sets the cap at 11/day.
  • 11 a day is comfortably under 100 in 24 hours.
  • 11 × 21 working days ≈ 231 a month — under 1,000 in 30 days.
  • ≈2,800 a year at that rate — under 10,000 in a year.
So no <ADV> prefix is required, and the subject line stays readable. Sender identity, UEN, postal address and a one-click opt-out still go on every send anyway.
⚠ The cap is a legal setting, not a growth dial. One enthusiastic day at 150 sends makes <ADV> legally required on that subject matter — and an <ADV> subject line kills the open rate. Enforce the cap in the sending script, not by discipline, and treat any request to raise it as a decision that needs sign-off.

Sources: PDPC Advisory Guidelines on requiring consent for marketing purposes · Spam Control Act 2007, Second Schedule · IRAS GST InvoiceNow requirement. Confirm with counsel before any high-volume unsolicited campaign.

The cold sequence — four touches over 18 days, then stop

1

Day 1 · The date they have not checked

Names their industry, says InvoiceNow start dates run on turnover bands, asks one question. No pitch, no attachment, no link beyond the signature.

Your InvoiceNow date — which band is [Company] in?

2

Day 4 · Proof from a business like theirs

One paragraph and one link to the closest published Singapore proposal or showcase. Shows a scoped Odoo build instead of describing one.

What this looked like for a Singapore [industry] firm

3

Day 11 · The cheapest next step

Offers the 20-minute readiness call on the Odoo booking page. No charge, no obligation, and they leave knowing their likely band.

20 minutes, and you will know your own timeline

4

Day 18 · Close the loop

Two lines. Offers the readiness checklist with no reply required and says this is the last email on the subject. Non-responders exit permanently.

Closing the file — checklist attached if useful

Lane 2, using what already exists

Three HTML templates are built, tested and live at technextsg.github.io/technext-email-marketing — intro/service offer, Odoo ERP, and a reusable monthly "TechNext Insights" shell. Each has a plain-text alternate. No new design work is needed, only Singapore copy. Newsletter goes out monthly, second week, Tuesday or Wednesday morning SGT — hold the slot for three months before testing anything.

The list has to be built, and only three things build it.
  • The free readiness audit — they get a real one-page assessment, we get a dated opt-in.
  • The InvoiceNow readiness checklist as a gated download.
  • The park-it offer at the end of every warm sequence: "shall I move you to the monthly note instead?"
All three need a working form with consent logging on technext.asia. That is the one build dependency this page carries.
