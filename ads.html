Facebook ads for Odoo, in Singapore.

One ad, one offer, one landing page, optimised for a booked demo rather than a click. Small enough to run properly, funded long enough to produce an answer.

The proposal: $1,000 a month for three months — $3,000. $800 to cold prospecting, $200 to retargeting, both optimising for a lead on technext.asia/lp/. Planned outcome: about 34 leads and 7 demos a month, and roughly two signed clients a quarter at ~$1,400 of media each — against a first-year contract near SGD 13,000.

1 Strategy

Four decisions. Everything else follows from them.

1

Optimise for leads, not clicks

The objective is Leads, with a Lead event firing on the landing page's confirmation. Optimising for traffic buys the people most likely to load a page, which is a different group from the people most likely to book a demo.

2

One ad, not five

A $1,000 budget split across several ads teaches nothing about any of them. One creative carries the whole cold budget until it has earned a comparison.

3

Send traffic to /lp/

technext.asia/lp/ is the Odoo landing page — free one-hour demo, five-field form, privacy policy linked. It does the qualifying an Instant Form cannot.

4

Sell the demo, not Odoo

Nobody buys an ERP from an ad. The ad's only job is a free 30-minute demo loaded with the prospect's own products and prices. That offer is the entire pitch.

Why Facebook and not LinkedIn or Google. Singapore SME owners are on Facebook personally, and Meta's small business owners and Page admins behaviours are the strongest B2B proxies available at this budget. LinkedIn's minimum viable spend in this market is several times higher, and Google search volume for "Odoo partner Singapore" is too thin to fund a channel on its own.

Budget barely moves cost per client. It moves how many, and how fast you know. Media per client is set by the funnel rates, not the budget — it lands near $1,400 whether the spend is $1,000 or $2,000 a month. So the question is not whether a client is affordable, it is how quickly they should arrive and how soon the numbers can be trusted. $1,000 buys about two clients a quarter and a reliable cost per lead by week 12.
Why three months, not a short burst. Meta needs volume before it delivers predictably — around 50 conversions per ad set per week, which at $29 a lead would cost far more than this plan. So the cold ad set will run in extended learning and weekly figures will bounce around. The answer is time rather than money: keep the spend on one ad set so the data lands in one place, and let it run twelve weeks. About 100 leads over the quarter is enough to trust. Stopping in week 3 pays for the expensive part and skips the answer.
⚠ Confirm the account currency before approving. The ad account displays a bare $. If it bills in USD, $1,000 a month is about SGD 1,340 — check Billing → Payment methods and restate the figure in one currency on the approval.

4 Timeline & KPIs

Two weeks to build, twelve to run.

WhenDo
Before launchPixel and Conversions API live, Lead firing on the /lp/ confirmation only. Creative exported at 1080×1350 and 1080×1080. Custom audiences built. Naming and UTMs set: TN | Meta | Odoo | Leads-SG | 2026-09. One real test lead submitted end to end.
Week 1–2Live. Do not touch it — the ad set is in learning. Answer every comment publicly; "how much?" questions do real conversion work.
Week 3–4First read on cost per lead. Replace the planning assumptions with actuals and recalculate before changing anything.
Week 5–8Introduce a second creative against the first. Refresh the retargeting copy — that pool fatigues fastest.
Week 9–12Judge on signed clients. Two or more: raise to $2,000. One: hold. Zero: stop spending and fix the demo, not the ad.
