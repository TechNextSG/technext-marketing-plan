Facebook ads for Odoo, in Singapore.

One ad, one offer, one landing page, optimised for a booked demo rather than a click. Small enough to run properly, funded long enough to produce an answer.

The proposal: $1,000 a month for three months — $3,000. $800 to cold prospecting, $200 to retargeting, both optimising for a lead on technext.asia/lp/. Planned outcome, deliberately stated as ranges: 3–4 qualified leads a month at most, which is 2–3 held demos, and one signed client a quarter — two in a good one, at $1,500–3,000 of media each against a first-year contract near SGD 13,000.

1 Strategy

Four decisions. Everything else follows from them.

1

Optimise for leads, not clicks

The objective is Leads, with a Lead event firing on the landing page's confirmation. Optimising for traffic buys the people most likely to load a page, which is a different group from the people most likely to book a demo.

2

One ad, not five

A $1,000 budget split across several ads teaches nothing about any of them. One creative carries the whole cold budget until it has earned a comparison.

3

Send traffic to /lp/

technext.asia/lp/ is the Odoo landing page — free one-hour demo, five-field form, privacy policy linked. It does the qualifying an Instant Form cannot.

4

Sell the call, not Odoo

Nobody buys an ERP from an ad. The ad's only job is a booked free 30-minute discovery call — the offer now written on the artwork itself. The demo on their own products is the step after, not the thing being sold here.

Why Facebook and not LinkedIn or Google. Singapore SME owners are on Facebook personally, and Meta's small business owners and Page admins behaviours are the strongest B2B proxies available at this budget. LinkedIn's minimum viable spend in this market is several times higher, and Google search volume for "Odoo partner Singapore" is too thin to fund a channel on its own.

The ad copy

The artwork asks a question and offers an appointment, so the text answers the question and says what the appointment is.

Primary text — cold

Most Odoo conversations start with a feature list. This one starts with your business.

Thirty minutes on how you quote, invoice and track stock today, and where Odoo would actually save time — POS, inventory, CRM and accounting in one system, fitted to the way you already work. No slide deck, and no licence to buy.

If it looks like a fit, the follow-up demo runs on your own products and prices. TechNext is an Odoo Ready Partner in Singapore — book a free 30-minute discovery call.

The first line is 86 characters, so the whole hook is visible before Meta's "See more" cut on mobile. It deliberately repeats neither the artwork's headline nor its offer — the picture asks the question and names the ask, so the text is free to say why this call is different from every other vendor call.

⚠ Three things to hold to before launch. It is "Odoo Ready Partner" — the tier Odoo's own directory lists; "Certified Partner" is not one of their company tiers. The artwork promises 30 minutes, free, so the call has to stay free and stay roughly 30 minutes. And the copy promises a follow-up demo on the prospect's own products, so somebody must own preparing that. No client counts, no implementation timelines, no performance claims until there is documented proof.

Three or four qualified conversations a month is the honest ceiling. Not because the ad is weak, but because that is what $1,000 buys against Singapore B2B costs once the form fill and the reachable-and-in-market filter have both taken their cut. It is a small number, and it is the right one to plan staffing and expectations around. Anyone promising thirty leads a month at this budget is quoting raw form fills, not conversations worth having.

Is it worth it? One client pays for the quarter about four times over. Six to nine held demos across three months, closing at 10–15%, is one signed client — two if it goes well. At a first-year contract near SGD 13,000 against $3,000 of media, a single win returns roughly 4×. The honest flip side: six to nine demos is not many rolls of the dice, so a quarter could close nothing. That is precisely why the spend is capped at three months — the downside is a known $3,000, not an open commitment.
Budget moves volume and speed of learning, not cost per client. Media per client is set by the funnel rates, so it stays in the $1,500–3,000 band whether the spend is $1,000 or $2,000 a month. Doubling the budget does not make clients cheaper; it makes them arrive sooner and the numbers trustworthy earlier.
Why three months, not a short burst. Meta needs volume before it delivers predictably — far more per week than this budget will ever produce — so the cold ad set will run in extended learning and weekly figures will bounce around. The answer is time rather than money: keep the spend on one ad set so the data lands in one place, and let it run twelve weeks. Thirty to forty form submissions over the quarter is thin, but it is enough to separate a working offer from a broken one. Stopping in week 3 pays for the expensive part and skips the answer.
⚠ Confirm the account currency before approving. The ad account displays a bare $. If it bills in USD, $1,000 a month is about SGD 1,340 — check Billing → Payment methods and restate the figure in one currency on the approval.

4 Timeline & KPIs

Two weeks to build, twelve to run.

WhenDo

Week 1–2Live. Do not touch it — the ad set is in learning. Answer every comment publicly; "how much?" questions do real conversion work.
Week 3–4First read on cost per lead. Replace the planning assumptions with actuals and recalculate before changing anything.
Week 5–8Run the first A/B test. Two candidates, in order: a Singaporean face in place of the stock portrait, and a version with the painted button and contact line removed. Refresh the retargeting copy too — that pool fatigues fastest.
Week 9–12Judge on signed clients. Two: raise to $2,000. One: on plan — hold at $1,000 and run another quarter. Zero, but demos were held: the offer or the demo is the problem, not the ad. Zero, and no demos: stop spending.
