2 What the numbers say

Four readings, in the order they need fixing.

Finding 1 The objective is wrong, and it is the whole problem

Every campaign optimises for landing page views, Page visits or messaging conversations. None optimises for a lead. Meta delivers exactly what it is asked for, so it has spent $223 finding the people most likely to load a page — which is a different population from the people most likely to book a demo.

That is also why the CPM is $3.40. A Singapore B2B CPM that low is not a bargain; it is the price of low-intent inventory. Switching to a lead objective will raise the CPM several times over, and that is the correct trade.

Finding 2 518 landing page views, and nobody knows what they did

The ads point at technext.asia/lp/, which is a proper Odoo landing page with a five-field form and a privacy policy link. But there is no confirmed Lead pixel event on its confirmation state, so the funnel goes dark at exactly the moment it starts to matter.

The only conversion signal in the whole account is 4 messaging conversations from the three messaging-optimised campaigns — $41.82 of spend, so about $10.46 a conversation. That is the single most useful number here, and it came from campaigns that are now switched off.

Finding 3 The two live ads differ by 4×, and the budget is split evenly

"Accelerating Cloud-based" gets a landing page view for $0.25. "5 Different Spreadsheets" pays $1.06 for the same thing — a 4× gap on comparable spend ($43 vs $40) and comparable impressions. Its 0.37% view rate against the other's 1.33% says the creative or the hook is not landing, not that the audience is wrong.

Finding 4 $7.70 a day cannot exit the learning phase

Meta wants roughly 50 optimisation events per ad set per week to leave learning and deliver predictably. Nine campaigns have been run at $2/day boosts and $100 lifetime budgets, and the whole account has spent $223 ever. Every result on this page was produced by an ad set that never stabilised — which is why the same creative can look 4× better or worse than its neighbour without that meaning very much.

⚠ Eight unpublished drafts are sitting in the account. Ads Manager shows "Review and publish (8)". Publish them deliberately or discard them — a drawer of half-built campaigns is how budget gets spent by accident.

3 The one ad, and the one change

Consolidate onto the winner, change what it optimises for, leave everything else alone.

1

Keep "Accelerating Cloud-based"

It buys attention 4× cheaper than the other live ad on comparable spend. Pause "5 Different Spreadsheets" rather than deleting it — it becomes the challenger once the winner has a real cost per lead to beat.

2

Change the objective to Leads

Same creative, same audience, same destination. This is the only change that turns the spend from traffic into pipeline, and it is a five-minute edit.

3

Fire Lead on the /lp/ confirmation

Pixel plus Conversions API, on the confirmation state only. Firing on the form page inflates conversions and sends the algorithm after the wrong people.

4

Fund one ad set, not nine

One campaign, one ad set, one ad, at a budget that clears the learning phase. Nine campaigns at $2/day is nine ad sets all stuck in learning at once.

5 What a client costs

Even the pessimistic case works. Paying $4,500 in media for a SGD 13,000 first-year contract is still roughly 3× before renewal, and Odoo clients renew. That headroom is the whole argument for $50/day over $7.70 — the deal size can carry the learning phase, and the current budget cannot.
⚠ The close rate is the assumption most likely to be wrong. 12% of demos is a reasonable planning figure for B2B software, but TechNext has no measured close rate on ad-sourced leads — the account has never produced a tracked lead. If it turns out to be 5%, the planning case becomes $5,400 a client and the right response is to fix the demo, not the ad. Judge this on cost per signed client, not cost per landing page view.
